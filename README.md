# Design and Fabrication of a Parabolic Solar Water Heater Using Copper Pipe

## Project Overview

This project focuses on the design and fabrication of a mechanically operated parabolic solar water heater using a copper pipe as the heat-absorbing receiver.

The system uses a parabolic reflector to concentrate sunlight onto a copper pipe positioned near the focal region. Water flowing through the copper pipe absorbs the heat and produces hot water.

The prototype works without electrical heating, sensors, electronic controllers, or motors.

## Objectives

- Design a parabolic solar reflector for water heating.
- Position a copper pipe at the focal region.
- Fabricate a simple mechanical solar water heating system.
- Study the temperature rise of water under sunlight.
- Evaluate the thermal performance of the prototype.
- Develop a low-cost renewable-energy-based water heating system.

## Working Principle

The parabolic reflector concentrates approximately parallel solar rays toward the focal region.

The copper pipe is positioned at the focal region of the reflector. Solar radiation reflected from the surface heats the copper pipe, and the heat is transferred to the water flowing through it.

### Heat Transfer Path

Sunlight  
↓  
Reflective Sheet  
↓  
Copper Pipe  
↓  
Pipe Wall  
↓  
Water

### Water Flow

Water Source  
↓  
Inlet  
↓  
Heated Copper Pipe  
↓  
Outlet  
↓  
Hot Water

## Parabolic Design

The reflector profile is based on:

x² = 4fy

where:

- `f` = focal length
- `x` = horizontal distance from the vertex
- `y` = vertical depth from the vertex

The focal length used in the project is:

**f = 73 mm**

Therefore:

**x² = 292y**

The focal point is approximately **73 mm above the vertex**.

## Prototype Development

The prototype was fabricated using a wooden supporting frame, curved metal sheet, reflective sheet, copper pipe and mechanical fittings.

The main fabrication sequence was:

1. Prepare the wooden supporting frame.
2. Form and mount the curved metal sheet.
3. Fix the reflective sheet smoothly.
4. Position the copper pipe at the focal region.
5. Connect the water inlet and outlet.
6. Check the structure and water connections.
7. Perform outdoor testing.

## Testing

The prototype was tested under direct sunlight by monitoring:

- Inlet water temperature
- Outlet water temperature
- Temperature difference
- Exposure duration
- Weather condition
- Water quantity/flow
- Solar irradiance when available

### Temperature Rise Test

| Trial | Time | Inlet Temperature | Outlet Temperature | ΔT |
|------|------|-------------------|--------------------|----|
| 1 | 15 min | 28°C | 34°C | 6°C |
| 2 | 30 min | 28°C | 39°C | 11°C |
| 3 | 45 min | 29°C | 45°C | 16°C |
| 4 | 60 min | 29°C | 51°C | 22°C |

The test results showed an increase in outlet water temperature with increased exposure time.

## Thermal Performance

The report uses the following equations:

### Useful Heat

Q = m × Cp × ΔT

### Thermal Power

Q̇ = ṁ × Cp × (Tout - Tin)

### Efficiency

η = Q̇ / (I × A)

where:

- `m` = mass of water
- `Cp` = specific heat capacity of water
- `ΔT` = temperature difference
- `ṁ` = mass flow rate
- `I` = solar irradiance
- `A` = collector area

## Safety

- Do not look directly at the concentrated focal region.
- Avoid touching the heated copper pipe.
- Use heat-resistant gloves during handling.
- Keep combustible materials away from the focal region.
- Check all water connections for leakage.
- Ensure the reflector and pipe are structurally stable.
- Keep children and unauthorized persons away from the working area.
